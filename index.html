Gemara reconstruction · 21b:1–4

Can future despair count now?

Imagine an ordinary lost object without an identifying mark. The owner would despair upon learning of the loss—but has not yet learned. Abaye requires awareness; Rava counts the anticipated response now.

Open SVG

O1–O3 are scope statements, deliberately unconnected. They define where the dispute applies; they are not premises proving either side. The red arrow records the conflict between the positions.

Gemara reconstruction · 21b:7

A reply can change the facts.

The Mishnah allows the finder to keep scattered coins. The anonymous objection asks how that can hold if the owner is still unaware. The answer invokes Rabbi Yitzhak’s habitual purse checking.

Open SVG

Follow C5 → C6 → C2: the reply attacks the assumed absence of awareness. It leaves the Mishnah intact. The overview adds the challenge’s attack on Abaye (C4 → O5). No undercut is asserted.

Gemara reconstruction · 22b:5–7

The exception illuminates the rule.

The verse excludes loss inaccessible to everyone. The Gemara then compares the permitted and prohibited categories, including objects with and without identifying marks.

Open SVG

R4 makes the temporal qualification visible: this concerns the owner’s unawareness before pickup (Rashi). R5 reconstructs the comparison; river permission alone does not refute Rava. The overview shows R6 attacking Rava (O7).
