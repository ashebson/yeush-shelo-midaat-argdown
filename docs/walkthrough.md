# Four minutes with Judd

## 0:00–0:45 · Establish the question

Open `index.html`, on **The dispute**. “Someone loses an ordinary object with no identifying mark. When they find out, they will give up hope of recovery. But they do not yet know. Can that future response count now?”

Point to Abaye and Rava. Then point out the three scope nodes: the disputed unmarked case; agreed treatment of marked objects; the separate Torah-permitted sea/river exception. Avoid calling every object in a river exempt.

## 0:45–1:45 · Show why the edge target matters

Switch to **The coins challenge**. Read C1 and C2 together. The Mishnah permits taking scattered coins, while the question assumes the owner is still unaware. C3 is our explicit bridge; C4 collects the objection to Abaye.

Trace C5 → C6 → C2. “The reply does not deny the Mishnah. It changes the account of what the owner knows: people check their purses.” Explain that C6's timing expansion follows Rashi. The reply attacks a premise; we have not represented it as an undercut of an inference.

## 1:45–3:00 · Make the final interpretive step inspectable

Switch to **The decisive comparison**. R1–R2 distinguish ordinary recoverable loss from loss inaccessible to everyone. Then point to R3: the Gemara compares the permitted and prohibited categories regarding identifying marks.

“River permission alone does not establish the result. R4 exposes the timing qualification, following Rashi; R5 records our reconstruction of the comparison. It reaches ordinary unmarked loss before awareness.” Show R7's explicit refutation of Rava and R8's ruling for Abaye.

Open the source-ledger entries R3, R4 and R5 to distinguish source wording, commentary and reconstruction. This is where discussion is most useful.

## 3:00–4:00 · Invite a concrete revision

Switch to **Full overview** to show the cross-episode attacks. Zoom if needed. Then return to the cleaner episode view.

Ask: “Which edge would you change? Is C3 the right bridge? Does R5 expose enough of the comparison? Which omitted episode would best test this representation?”

Emphasize: this is 22 nodes from three selected episodes, not every line from 21b–22b. The text's ruling is recorded; no algorithm computes halakhah. The next demonstration could add figs/olives, showing a challenge to the expectation of eventual despair.
